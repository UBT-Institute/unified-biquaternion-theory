## The action, not commutation, determines charge

For a psi-independent electromagnetic gauge parameter, every Fourier coefficient has the same declared left charge. Being real or having zero Fourier index does not make a nonzero field invariant. Central conjugation would leave the ENTIRE algebra invariant and could not charge just the other components. The old theorem therefore establishes neither neutrality nor a massless photon.

\[
\Theta'=e^{i\alpha}\Theta,\qquad \left.\frac{d}{d\alpha}(e^{i\alpha}v)\right|_{\alpha=0}=iv\ne0\quad(v\ne0),\qquad e^{i\alpha}ve^{-i\alpha}=v.
\qquad \theta'_n=e^{i\alpha}\theta_n.
\]

## No mixed linear neutral/charged split under these hypotheses

Fix the recorded Lorentz congruence action on the original matrix carrier and allow real-linear internal transformations commuting with it. Write the field as two Hermitian four-vectors. The commutant of the real Lorentz vector representation is the real scalars: commuting with spatial rotations first gives a temporal scalar and a common spatial scalar; commuting with boosts equates them and excludes temporal-spatial mixing. Consequently the full real commutant on the two copies is the real two-by-two matrix algebra.

\[
X=U+iV,\quad U=U^\dagger,\quad V=V^\dagger,\quad X\mapsto SXS^\dagger,\quad S\in SL(2,\mathbb C),
\qquad \operatorname{End}_{\rm Lorentz}(\mathbb B_{\mathbb R})=M_2(\mathbb R)\otimes I_4.
\]

<!-- BILINGUAL-UNIT: u1-neutrality.compact -->

## Compact phase representations have no partial fixed subspace here

Average any positive inner product over the compact group. In an orthonormal multiplicity basis a continuous U(1) generator is a real skew two-by-two matrix. Periodicity makes its rotation weight an integer. A zero weight leaves all components neutral; every nonzero weight has an invertible generator and no nonzero fixed linear vector. A nonorthogonal basis does not change that kernel. For the declared central phase the weight is one and the two Hermitian parts rotate into one another. They cannot simply be called a neutral sector and a charged sector. The theorem is confined to this carrier, Lorentz action and commuting real-linear internal symmetry; composites, jets and other derived representations are outside it. It is not a no-go theorem for every UBT completion.

\[
J\sim\begin{pmatrix}0&-k\\ k&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes I_4,\quad k\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad k=0:\ \ker J=\mathbb R^8,\qquad k\ne0:\ \ker J=\{0\}.
\quad U'=\cos\alpha\,U-\sin\alpha\,V,\quad V'=\sin\alpha\,U+\cos\alpha\,V.
\]

## A neutral composite of the same field

There is a constructive alternative within the same carrier: study an already classified invariant composite rather than declare a charged linear component neutral. Quaternion sharp is complex-linear and equals the two-by-two adjugate. The invariant below is real and phase neutral, but indefinite, so it is NOT a positive physical norm or an automatically healthy scalar. A nonzero expectation of an invariant composite does not by itself determine gauge-symmetry realization. No independent scalar is introduced.

\[
\mathcal H[X]=\operatorname{Tr}(X^\sharp X^\dagger),\quad X^\sharp=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I-X,\quad
\mathcal H[e^{i\alpha}SXS^\dagger]=\mathcal H[X],\quad
\mathcal H[U+iV]=2(u^2+v^2),\quad u^2=(u^0)^2-\boldsymbol u^2.
\]

## Neutrality does not create an independent wave

The linearization of the composite is the stated real-linear functional of the original fluctuation. It vanishes at a zero field background. In a zero-mean, phase-invariant Gaussian vacuum comparator a bilinear therefore has a two-particle connected response rather than an elementary single-particle pole. At a nonzero background its possible poles are projections of the constrained original propagator; the background must also pass the earlier charge test. In an interacting theory a neutral bound-state pole is possible, but must be calculated. Replacing this correlator by a freely assigned scalar propagator would assume the missing result.

\[
\delta\mathcal H[X_0;\delta X]=\operatorname{Tr}[(\delta X)^\sharp X_0^\dagger+X_0^\sharp(\delta X)^\dagger],
\qquad \delta\mathcal H[0;\delta X]=0.
\]
