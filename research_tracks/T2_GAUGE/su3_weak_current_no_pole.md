# Weak-coupling no-pole theorem for a triplet adjoint current

**Status:** exact free-field spectral statement plus perturbative benchmark.
It separates an induced background \(F^2\) term from a genuine composite
massless gluon pole.

## 1. Candidate adjoint current

Suppose, for the sake of the composite-colour test, that a charged complex
triplet
\[
\phi\in\mathbb C^3
\]
with generators \(T^a=\lambda_a/2\) is present in a fixed-background
effective sector.

The global adjoint current is
\[
\boxed{
J_\mu^a
=
i\,
\phi^\dagger T^a
\stackrel{\leftrightarrow}{\partial_\mu}
\phi.
}
\]

It is bilinear in the elementary triplet field.

## 2. Free Fock-space consequence

In a free quadratic theory, the field expansion is linear in creation and
annihilation operators.

Therefore the normal-ordered bilinear current contains terms of the schematic
types
\[
a^\dagger a,\qquad
b^\dagger b,\qquad
a^\dagger b^\dagger,\qquad
ba.
\]

Acting on the vacuum, only the pair-creation term contributes:
\[
J_\mu^a|0\rangle
\sim
a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle.
\]

Hence the current has no matrix element from the vacuum to a one-particle
elementary state:
\[
\boxed{
\langle {\rm one\ particle}|J_\mu^a|0\rangle=0.
}
\]

Its Kallen--Lehmann spectral measure therefore begins with the two-particle
continuum, not an isolated one-vector delta function.

## 3. Massless case

If the triplet is massless, the two-particle threshold starts at
\[
s=0.
\]

The renormalized nonlocal part of the perturbative current correlator has the
transverse form
\[
\langle J_\mu^a(p)J_\nu^b(-p)\rangle
=
\delta^{ab}
(p_\mu p_\nu-p^2\eta_{\mu\nu})
\left[
c_R\ln\!\left(\frac{-p^2}{\mu^2}\right)
+\text{local terms}
\right],
\]
where \(c_R>0\) is proportional to the representation trace normalization.

As \(p^2\to0\),
\[
p^2\ln(-p^2)\to0.
\]

This is a branch-cut/continuum singularity, not a simple massless
one-particle pole of the form
\[
\frac{Z}{p^2+i0}
\left(
\eta_{\mu\nu}
-\frac{p_\mu p_\nu}{p^2}
\right).
\]

## 4. Massive case

For triplet mass \(m>0\), the continuum begins at
\[
s=4m^2.
\]

Again there is no one-vector pole in the free current correlator.

A pole below threshold would require a bound state generated by interactions.

## 5. Consequence for induced Yang--Mills calculations

Integrating a weakly coupled charged triplet in a **background** connection can
generate a local or running \(F^2\) term in the effective action.

That fact alone does not imply that the original single-Theta Hilbert space
contains a new independent massless vector particle.

Thus the logical chain is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{charged loop}
\Rightarrow
\text{background }F^2
}
\]

but not

\[
\boxed{
\text{charged loop}
\Rightarrow
\text{composite massless gluon pole}.
}
\]

The second implication needs a new dynamical statement.

## 6. What would be sufficient

A genuine composite gluon requires an exact interacting correlator with an
isolated transverse pole,
\[
\langle J_\mu^aJ_\nu^b\rangle
\supset
\delta^{ab}
\frac{Z}{p^2+i0}
\left(
\eta_{\mu\nu}
-\frac{p_\mu p_\nu}{p^2}
\right),
\qquad
Z>0,
\]
or an equivalent gauge-invariant formulation.

Producing such a pole from a bilinear current requires nonperturbative or
critical dynamics beyond the weakly coupled Gaussian triplet.

The pole must then also satisfy:
- the eightfold adjoint degeneracy;
- non-Abelian Ward/Slavnov--Taylor identities;
- universal self-coupling;
- absence of negative-residue states;
- correct matter coupling and running.

## 7. UBT consequence

The present one-loop heat-kernel and KK calculations are therefore best
interpreted as **background-response benchmarks**.

They constrain a possible emergent gauge sector but do not yet derive its
particle spectrum.

Preserving the one-fundamental-field architecture now requires a genuinely
strong/collective mechanism capable of generating new vector poles in exact
Theta composite correlators.
