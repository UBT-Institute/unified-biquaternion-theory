# Minimal bimodule intersection with u(1,3): exactly Lorentz plus one central phase

**Status:** exact linear-algebra/Lie-algebra theorem for the minimal two-sided
biquaternion operator class.

## 1. Operator class

Let
\[
\mathbb B=\mathbb C\otimes\mathbb H
\cong\mathbb C^4
\]
in the coefficient basis
\[
(1,I,J,K).
\]

For \(A,B\in\mathbb B\), define
\[
T_{A,B}(X)=AX-XB
=
L_A X-R_B X.
\]

The map
\[
(A,B)\mapsto L_A-R_B
\]
has complex kernel
\[
(A,B)=(c\,1,c\,1),
\qquad c\in\mathbb C,
\]
so its operator image has complex dimension seven.

Use the Lorentz-Hermitian coefficient metric
\[
G=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).
\]

## 2. Exact u(1,3) intersection

Impose
\[
T^\dagger G+GT=0.
\]

Solving the resulting real linear system exactly gives
\[
\boxed{
\dim_{\mathbb R}
\left(
\operatorname{im}(L-R)\cap\mathfrak u(1,3)
\right)
=7.
}
\]

A basis is
\[
J_i=\frac12(L_{e_i}-R_{e_i}),
\]
\[
K_i=\frac{i}{2}(L_{e_i}+R_{e_i}),
\qquad
e_i\in\{I,J,K\},
\]
together with
\[
Q=i\,\mathbf1_4.
\]

The central generator \(Q\) commutes with all six \(J_i,K_i\).

## 3. The traceless intersection is exactly the Lorentz algebra

Imposing also
\[
\operatorname{tr}T=0
\]
removes \(Q\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\dim_{\mathbb R}
\left(
\operatorname{im}(L-R)\cap\mathfrak{su}(1,3)
\right)
=6.
}
\]

The exact commutators are
\[
[J_i,J_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}J_k,
\]
\[
[J_i,K_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}K_k,
\]
\[
[K_i,K_j]=-\epsilon_{ijk}J_k.
\]

Hence
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}(L-R)\cap\mathfrak{su}(1,3)
\cong
\mathfrak{so}(1,3).
}
\]

This is stronger than a dimension count: the norm-preserving traceless part of
the minimal two-sided biquaternion operator class is precisely the Lorentz Lie
algebra.

## 4. Intersection with the candidate colour stabilizer

At the reference timelike direction \(e_0=1\), the fibre
\[
E_{e_0}=\mathbb C\operatorname{-span}\{I,J,K\}
\]
has candidate stabilizer \(\mathfrak{su}(3)\).

Inside the minimal bimodule intersection, only
\[
J_1,J_2,J_3
\]
preserve \(e_0\).  Restricted to \(E_{e_0}\), they generate the real rotation
subalgebra
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak{so}(3)
=
\operatorname{span}
\{i\lambda_2,i\lambda_5,i\lambda_7\}
\subset\mathfrak{su}(3).
}
\]

The five quadrupole/Gell-Mann directions are absent.

Thus the exact result is
\[
\boxed{
\text{minimal norm-preserving bimodule}
\Rightarrow
\text{Lorentz }6
\text{ and colour-stabilizer }SO(3),
\text{ not full colour }SU(3).
}
\]

## 5. The extra u(1) is a phase, not the missing colour sector

The seventh \(u(1,3)\) direction is
\[
Q=i\mathbf1_4.
\]

It is the central complex phase.  It is not a traceless colour generator and
does not supply any of the five missing \(SU(3)/SO(3)\) directions.

Therefore enlarging from \(SU(1,3)\) to \(U(1,3)\) does not repair the colour
deficit.

## 6. Exact tetrad consequence at a central timelike vacuum

Take a phase-normalized central vacuum
\[
\Theta_0=i r\,1,
\qquad r\ne0.
\]

Then
\[
J_i\Theta_0=0,
\]
while
\[
K_i\Theta_0=-r\,e_i
\]
(up to the chosen generator normalization), so the three \(K_i\) produce the
correct real spatial quaternion directions.

But
\[
Q\Theta_0
=
-r\,1,
\]
which is a **real scalar** direction.

The canonical Lorentz-real tetrad slice instead requires the time direction
to be proportional to
\[
i\,1.
\]

A real central dilation
\[
D=\mathbf1_4
\]
does give
\[
D\Theta_0=i r\,1,
\]
while preserving the three spatial directions generated by the \(K_i\).

However
\[
D^\dagger G+GD=2G\ne0,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
D\notin\mathfrak u(1,3).
}
\]

Thus the operator needed to supply the fourth canonical tetrad direction is
exactly a norm-changing dilation outside the norm-preserving minimal
intersection.

## 7. No phase choice removes the relative-phase obstruction

For a general central vacuum
\[
\Theta_0=r e^{i\phi}1,
\]
the central phase generator and all three \(K_i\) carry the same extra factor
\(i e^{i\phi}\) in their orbit directions.

The canonical Lorentz-real tetrad requires a relative factor of \(i\) between
the scalar time leg and the three real spatial legs.

No choice of the common vacuum phase \(\phi\) can make both:
- \(Q\Theta_0\) proportional to \(i\,1\), and
- \(K_i\Theta_0\) proportional to real \(I,J,K\).

Using the real dilation \(D\) instead of \(Q\) supplies precisely the missing
relative phase.

## 8. Architectural conclusion

The current minimal two-sided connection has a remarkably rigid structure:

\[
\boxed{
(L_A-R_B)\cap u(1,3)
=
so(1,3)\oplus u(1)_{\rm phase}.
}
\]

It already contains the physical Lorentz algebra and one central phase, but it
does **not** contain:
- full colour \(su(3)\);
- the five quadrupole colour generators;
- a norm-preserving operator that simultaneously supplies the canonical
  fourth tetrad leg at the central timelike vacuum.

Therefore a unified colour+GR completion must choose among:

1. retain the real central dilation, accepting that the defining jet transport
   leaves the exact constant-norm colour orbit;
2. enlarge the defining connection beyond minimal left/right multiplication to
   a richer composite subalgebra of
   \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb B)\);
3. derive colour in a genuinely separate collective bundle/IR sector rather
   than in the same minimal jet connection.

This theorem sharply separates the already successful Lorentz role of the
minimal bimodule from the still-open colour problem.
